I listened with one ear, and even I was drawn back to those days when the Red Ships were no more than a tale and my life had been almost secure if not happy. It was strangely soothing: the stones themselves were red, black and white, smoothly polished and pleasant to hold. The game involved each player randomly drawing stones from the pouch and then placing them on the intersections of lines on a patterned cloth. It was a game at once simple and complex. Each time I won a game, Kettle immediately introduced me to more complicated strategies. It engrossed me and freed my mind from memories or ponderings. When finally all the others were already drowsing in their sleeping skins, she set up a game on the board and bade me study it.</p> <p class="Indent">‘It can be won decisively in one move of a black stone,’ she told me. ‘But the solution is not easy to see.’</p> <p class="Indent">I stared at the game layout and shook my head. ‘How long did it take you to learn to play?’</p> <p class="Indent">She smiled to herself. ‘As a child, I was a fast learner.
