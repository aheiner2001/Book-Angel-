<h1 id="c33" class="chapter1"><a id="page745" class="calibre13"></a><a href="part0036.html#c33a" class="calibre13"><small class="calibre10">THIRTY-THREE</small></a></h1>
<h1 id="heading_id_2" class="subchapter"><a href="part0036.html#c33a" class="calibre13">Wolf Days</a></h1>
<p class="nonindent1"><i class="calibre11">The exercise for centring oneself is a simple one. Stop thinking of what you intend to do. Stop thinking of what you have just done. Then, stop thinking that you have stopped thinking of those things. Then you will find the Now, the time that stretches eternal, and is really the only time there is. Then, in that place, you will finally have time to be yourself</i>.</p>
<p class="nonindent">There is a cleanness to life that can be had when you but hunt and eat and sleep. In the end, no more than this is really needed by anyone. We ran alone, we the Wolf, and we lacked for nothing. We did not long for venison when a rabbit presented itself nor begrudge the ravens that came to pick through our leavings. Sometimes we remembered a different time and a different way. When we did, we wondered what had been so important about any of it. We did not kill what we could not eat, and we did not eat what we could not kill. Dusks and dawns were the best times for hunting, and other times were good for sleeping. Other than this, time had no meaning.</p>
<p class="Indent">For wolves, as for dogs, life is a briefer thing than for men, if you measure it by counting days and how many turns of a season one sees. But in two years, a cub wolf does all a man does in a score. He comes to the full of his strength and size, he learns all that is needful for him to be a hunter or a mate or a leader. The candle of his life burns briefer and brighter than a man’s. In a decade of years, he does all <a id="page746" class="hlink"></a>that a man does in five or six times that many. A year passes for a wolf as a decade does for a man. Time is no miser when one lives always in the now.</p>
<p class="Indent">So we knew the nights and the days, the hunger and the filling. Savage joys and surprises. Snatch up a mouse, fling it up, eat it down with a snap. So good. To start a rabbit, to pursue it as it dodges and circles, then suddenly, to stretch your stride and seize it in a flurry of snow and fur. The shake that snaps its neck, and then the leisure eating, the tearing open of its belly and nosing through the hot entrails, and then the thick meat of the haunches, the easy crunching of its backbone. Surfeit and sleep. And waken to hunt again.</p>
<p class="Indent">Chase a doe over pond ice, knowing we cannot make such a kill, but rejoicing in the hunt. When through the ice she goes, and we circle, circle, circle endlessly as she battles her hooves against the ice and finally clambers out, too weary to evade the teeth that slash her hamstrings, the fangs that close in her throat. Eating to satiation, not once, but twice from the carcass. A storm comes full of sleet to drive us to the den. Sleeping snug, nose to tail, while the wind flings icy rain and then snow about outside the den. Awake to pale light glistening in through a layer of snow. Dig out to snuff the clear cold day that is just fading. There is meat still on the doe, frozen red and sweet, ready to be dug from the snow. What can be more satisfying than to know of meat that is waiting for you?</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">We pause. No, the meat is waiting. We trot on.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come now. Come to me. I’ve meat for you</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">We’ve meat already. And closer.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Nighteyes. Changer. Heart of the Pack summons you</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">We pause again. Shake all over. This is not comfortable. And what is Heart of the Pack to us? He is not pack. He pushes us. There is meat closer. It is decided. We go to the <a id="page747" class="hlink"></a>pond’s edge. Here. Somewhere here. Ah. Dig down to her through the snow. The crows come to watch us, waiting for us to be finished.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Nighteyes. Changer. Come. Come now. Soon it will be too late</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">The meat is frozen, crisp and red. Turn our head to use our back teeth to scissor it from the bones. A crow flies down, lands on the snow nearby. Hop, hop. He cocks his head. For sport, we lunge at him, put him to flight again. Our meat, all of it. Days and nights of meat.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come. Please. Come. Please. Come soon, come now. Come back to us. You are needed. Come. Come</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">He does not go away. We put back our ears, but still we hear him, <i class="calibre11">come, come, come</i>. He steals the pleasure from the meat with his whining. Enough. We have eaten enough for now. We will go, just to still him.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Good. That’s good. Come to me, come to me</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">We go, trotting through the gathering darkness. A rabbit sits up suddenly, scampers away across the snow. Shall we? No. Belly is full. Trot on. Cross a man’s path, an open empty strip under the night sky. We fade across it swiftly, trot on through the woods that border it.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come to me. Come. Nighteyes, Changer, I summon you. Come to me</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">The forest ends. There is a cleared hillside below us, and beyond that a flat, bare place, shelterless under the night sky. Too open. The crusted snow is untracked, but at the bottom of the hill, there are humans. Two. Heart of the Pack digs while another watches. Heart of the Pack digs fast and hard. His breath smokes in the night. The other has a light, a too-bright light that shrinks the eye to behold. Heart of the Pack stops his digging. He looks up at us.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come</i>, he says. <i class="calibre11">Come</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">He jumps into the hole he has dug. There is black earth, frozen chunks of it, on top of the clean snow. He lands with <a id="page748" class="hlink"></a>a thud like deer antlers on a tree. He crouches and there is a tearing sound. He uses a tool that thuds and tears. We settle down to watch him, wrapping tail around to warm front feet. What has this to do with us? We are full, we could go sleep now. He looks up at us suddenly through the night.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Wait. A moment longer. Wait</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">He growls to the other, and that one holds the light to the hole. Heart of the Pack bends his back and the other reaches to help him. They drag something from the hole. The smell of it sets our hackles ajar. We turn, we leap to run, we circle, we cannot leave. There is a fear here, there is a danger, a threat of pain, of loneliness, of endings.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come. Come down to us here, come down. We need you now. It is time</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">This is not time. Time is always, is everywhere. You need us, but perhaps we do not want to be needed. We have meat, and a warm place to sleep, and even more meat for another time. With a full belly and a warm den, what else is to be needed? Yet. We will go closer. We will snuff it, we will see what it is that threatens and beckons. Belly to snow, tail low, we slink down the hill.</p>
<p class="Indent">Heart of the Pack sits in the snow holding it. He motions the other away, and that one steps back, back, back taking his painful light with him. Closer. The hill is beyond us now, bare, shelterless. It is a far run back to hiding if we are threatened. But nothing moves. There is only Heart of the Pack and that which he holds. It smells of blood. He shakes it, as if to worry off a piece of meat. Then he rubs at it, moving his hands like a bitch’s teeth go over a cub to rid it of fleas. We know the smell of it. Closer we come. Closer. It is but a leap away.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">What do you want?</i> we demand of him.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come back</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">We have come</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come back here. Changer</i>. He is insistent. <i class="calibre11">Come back to <a id="page749" class="hlink"></a>this</i>. He lifts an arm, shows up a hand. He shows us a head lolling on his shoulder.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">That?</i></p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">This. This is yours, Changer</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">It smells bad. It is spoiled meat, we do not want it. There is better meat by the pond than that</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come here. Come closer</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">This is not a good idea. We will come no closer. He looks at us and grips us with his eyes. He edges closer to us, bringing it with him. It flops in his arms.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Easy. Easy. This is yours, Changer. Come closer</i>.</p>
<p class="Indent">We snarl, but he does not look away. We cower, tail to belly, wanting to leave, but he is strong. He takes its hand and puts it on our head. He holds the scruff of our neck to still us.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Come back. You must come back</i>. He is so insistent.</p>
<p class="Indent">We cower down, digging claws into the snowy earth. Humping our back, we try to pull away, struggle to take one step backward. He still holds onto the scruff of our neck. We gather strength to wheel and break away.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Let him go, Nighteyes. He is not yours</i>. A hint of teeth in those words, his eyes stare at us too hard.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">He is not yours, either</i>, Nighteyes says.</p>
<p class="Indent"><i class="calibre11">Whose am I, then?</i></p>
<p class="Indent">A moment of teetering, of balancing between two worlds, two realities, two fleshes. Then a wolf wheels and flees, tail tucked, over the snow, running away alone, fleeing from too much strangeness. On top of a hill he stops, to point his nose at the sky and howl. Howl for the unfairness of it all.</p>
<p class="extract">I do not have a memory of that frozen graveyard that is my own. I have a sort of dream. I was wretchedly cold, stiff, and the raw taste of brandy burned, not just in my mouth, but all through me. Burrich and Chade would not leave me <a id="page750" class="hlink"></a>alone. They didn’t care how much they were hurting me, they just kept on rubbing my hands and feet, careless of the old bruises, the scabs on my arms. And every time I closed my eyes, Burrich would seize me and shake me like a rag. ‘Stay with me, Fitz,’ he kept saying. ‘Stay with me, stay with me. Come on, boy. You’re not dead. You’re not dead.’ Then suddenly he hugged me to him, his bearded face bristling against mine and his hot tears falling on my face. He rocked me back and forth, sitting in the snow at the edge of my grave. ‘You’re not dead, son. You’re not dead.’</p>
