All three women suddenly laughed together. ‘No wonder you were so anxious to have word of him. Did he know, before he left?’</p> <p class="Indent">‘I did not even suspect it then. I so long to tell him, to watch his face.’</p> <p class="Indent">‘You’re with child,’ I said stupidly. They all turned to look at me, and then burst out laughing anew.</p> <p class="Indent">‘It’s a secret, still,’ Kettricken cautioned me. ‘I want no rumours before the King has been told. I want to be the one to tell him.’</p> <p class="Indent">‘Of course not,’ I assured her. I did not tell her that the Fool already knew, and had known for days. Verity’s child, I thought to myself. A sudden strange shivering raced over me. The branching of the path that the Fool had seen, the sudden multiplying of possibilities. One factor emerged above all others: the sudden removal of Regal, pushed one more step away from the throne. One more small life standing between him and the power he craved. How little he would care for that.</p> <p class="Indent">‘Of course not,’ I repeated more heartily. ‘This news is best kept an absolute secret.’ For once it was out, I had no doubt that Kettricken would be in as much danger as her husband.</p> </body></html>
