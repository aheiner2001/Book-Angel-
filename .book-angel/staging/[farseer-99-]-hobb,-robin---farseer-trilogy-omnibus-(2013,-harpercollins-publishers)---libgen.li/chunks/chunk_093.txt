I did think of my king, and all I had sworn to him. I thought of Prince Verity and his bluff heartiness and open ways with me. I recalled old Chade and his slow smile when I had finally mastered some arcane bit of lore. Lady Patience and her maid Lacey, Fedwren and Hod, even Cook and Mistress Hasty the seamstress. There were not so many folk that had cared for me, but that made <a id="page15" class="hlink"></a>them more significant, not less. I would miss all of them if I never went back to Buckkeep. But what leaped up in me like an ember rekindled was my memory of Molly. And somehow, I found myself speaking of her to Burrich, and him just nodding as I spilled out the whole story.</p> <p class="Indent">When he did speak, he told me only that he had heard that the Beebalm Chandlery had closed when the old drunkard that owned it had died in debt. Are you crippled? Only if you decide so. But if you’re determined that you’re a cripple now, then perhaps you’ve no right to go and seek her out. I don’t think you’d want her pity. Did I dread him smirking over my trembling hands? Could I not smirk back at he who would never be king? A savage satisfaction welled up in me. Burrich was right. I had not lost. But I could make sure that Regal knew I had won.</p> <p class="Indent">If I had won against Regal, could I not win Molly as well? What stood between her and me? Jade? Molly, with her hair loose and blowing, Molly with her bright red skirts and cloak, bold as a red-robber bird, and eyes as bright. The thought of her sent a shiver down my spine. I smiled to myself, and then felt my lips set like a rictus, and the shiver became a shuddering. My body spasmed and the back <a id="page16" class="hlink"></a>of my head rebounded sharply off the bedstead. I cried out involuntarily, a gargling wordless cry.</p> <p class="Indent">In an instant Jonqui was there, calling Burrich back, and then they were both holding down my flailing limbs. The down of the feather bed cradled me, the blankets were soft and warm. I felt safe. For a moment, all was peaceful. I lay quiescent, almost feeling good.</p> <p class="Indent">‘Fitz?’ Burrich asked, leaning over me.</p> <p class="Indent">The world came back. I knew myself a mangled, pitiful thing, a puppet with half its strings tangled or a horse with a severed tendon. I wanted pity from none of them.</p> <p class="Indent">‘Burrich.’</p> <p class="Indent">He leaned closer over me. ‘It wasn’t that bad,’ he lied. ‘Just rest now. Tomorrow you will go back to Buckkeep. There are people and animals waiting for you there. You’re needed. It’s your home and your world. But it’s not mine. Not any more.’</p> <p class="Indent">He was silent for a long moment. ‘And what will you do?’</p> <p class="Indent">I shook my head. ‘That’s no longer your concern. No. Alone or wed to another, she’s better off now as she is.’</p> <p class="Indent">The silence stretched long between us. Jonqui was busy in a corner of the room, concocting yet another herbal draught that would do nothing for me. Burrich stood over me, black and lowering as a thundercloud. I knew how badly he wanted to shake me, how he longed to cuff the stubbornness from me. But he did not. Burrich did not hit cripples.</p> <p class="Indent">‘So,’ he said at last. ‘That leaves only your king. Somehow it was much more bearable to move forward into a life where no one would recall who I had once been. A life not pledged to someone else’s will. Not even my king’s. It was done. I lay back in my bed, and for the first time in weeks, I relaxed completely. Farewell, I thought wearily.
